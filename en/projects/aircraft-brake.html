RETURN TO MATRIX

Aircraft Brake System

Preliminary floating-caliper brake design: architecture, CAD integration and mechanical checks.

Mission Overview

Student engineering project focused on designing a full brake system for a light aircraft wheel. The scope covered the functional architecture, CAD integration, and key engineering checks without repeating full calculations.

Objective: deliver a compact, safe, and fully dismantlable assembly, from wheel pivot and bearings to disc, pads, floating caliper, key interface, and aircraft attachment.

02 · ARCHITECTURE

A compact, dismantlable assembly

The floating caliper was selected for compact packaging and simpler integration. Hydraulic pressure acts on the piston and pads; the caliper translates along its guide to clamp both sides of the disc. The wheel rotates around a fixed shaft, supported by two preloaded 32004X tapered roller bearings in an O arrangement.

Load transfer. The disc is connected to the wheel through its carrier. The caliper guide transfers the braking reaction to the structure; bearings support radial and axial loads. Bolted interfaces and the key receive separate engineering checks.
03 · ENGINEERING CHECKS

Checking components and interfaces

Bearings and preload

Remove internal play and check that the bearing arrangement remains seated in the sizing load case.

Fasteners and key

Check joint slip and local bearing pressure with M6 grade 8.8 fasteners and a standard key.

Shaft: bending and torsion

A linear-elastic analysis gives a calculated safety factor near 3 for the studied load case.

Disc: thermal balance

A simplified estimate of dissipated energy and heat transfer checks the allowable disc temperature.

04 · MANUFACTURING AND MAINTENANCE

Preparing manufacture and servicing

Bearing seats and bores are designed for turning, caliper features and keyways for milling, and fastener interfaces for drilling and tapping. The caliper uses two parts to remain machinable. Removing it from the guide exposes the pads; the slotted nut allows preload adjustment and wheel removal.

This remains a preliminary design. Checks use simplified models; no brake prototype or physical testing is documented. Thermal cycling, fatigue, vibration, tolerances and stresses in the caliper and guide require further study.
