01 · OBJECTIVE

Connect the test bench to the numerical model

A three-point bending beam and a fixed frame were loaded beyond the elastic range. Experimental curves, limit analysis and nonlinear finite-element results were then compared to identify yielding, plastic hinges and collapse loads.

02 · PHYSICAL TESTS

Observing yielding before modelling it

The frame load reaches 432 N. Stiffness falls from about 38 to 3 N/mm during loading, and three plastic hinges are observed. The complete quantitative beam-test data remain to be recovered.

03 · CALIBRATION

Relating material parameters to measurements

The supplied Python script is adapted to the measured geometries and material properties. Hardening parameters are then fitted to characteristic slopes and loads in the force–displacement curves: σᵧ = 315 MPa and E₁ = 2,150 MPa for the beam; σᵧ = 380 MPa and E₁ = 1,700 MPa for the frame.

Equations linking section, span and collapse

Vertically loaded frame

The lowest admissible mechanism gives 423 N, compared with 432 N measured.

Angle convention

α ana = 90 ° − α Abaqus

In Abaqus, 0° denotes vertical loading. Comparison uses the analytical angle and the absolute load.

Limit analysis uses virtual work and selects the smallest load among admissible mechanisms. The three hinges observed in the test are distinguished from the idealized mechanisms considered in the calculation.

08 · RESULTS AND LIMITATIONS

What the comparison supports

Plastic zones and the transition around 60° in the analytical convention help interpret the mechanisms. However, analytical curves and Abaqus points do not superpose satisfactorily, even after several angle-convention adjustments. Boundary conditions, modelling simplifications and mesh resolution are possible causes to investigate; their roles have not been established. Agreement of the vertical limit load with the test therefore does not validate the full angle study.
